**What this is:** the concepts, philosophy and theory of how THIS piece uses temporal notation — *"how standard notation is used in a
temporal context/mapping"* (LG-120). Not the Earle Brown genre that leans to graphic notation.

## §1 The name

- **His term: "temporal notation"** — kept for now (*"lets stick with temporal notation for now"*), defined once in the performance
  notes as **horizontal distance = time**.
- **The literature's terms** (RUNNING_LOG §536 · §537, from the AI's memory, unverified): **proportional notation** (Stone · Read) ·
  time-space / spatial notation · Brown's "time notation" · clock-time notation, Cage's time brackets · on a screen: animated notation /
  screen score (Hope · Vickery).
- **Against "metric notation"** the best pair is **metric ↔ proportional** (§538); the idea underneath is Boulez's striated ↔ smooth time.
- **Literature:** Stone, *Music Notation in the Twentieth Century* (1980) · Read, *Modern Rhythmic Notation* (1978) · Karkoschka,
  *Notation in New Music* (1966 / 1972) · Cardew, "Notation — Interpretation, etc.", *Tempo* 58 (1961) · Vickery, *Organised Sound*
  17/2 (2012) · Hope, *Computer Music Journal* 41/3 (2017). **Unverified — titles, dates and issues from memory.**

## §3 Concepts and theories — SHORT NOTES (his list, organized; no order)

*From LG-120; his phrases in italics.*

- **C1 — What short notes DO in this system, against traditional notation (TN).** In TN the *"notehead/stems/beams"* carry onset AND
  duration together; the beams carry the GROUPING and the RATIOS. *"In my system so far I have focused on onset precision ie gcs and
  precise duration ie duration lines."* → the system carries two DIGITAL facts (the onset, the span) and one ANALOG flow (the curves);
  what it does not yet carry is the RHYTHM of a group — the ratios between onsets, read as a shape.
- **C2 — The context.** *"The faster phrases in section 2 in the EH."* (Section 2 = 289 … 427 s; the long tones are done — PLAN § `2m`.)
- **C3 — The aim: a PHRASE-BASED notation.** *"Instead of just indicating an onset or a long held tone I want to notate a phrase in the
  temporal notation with good rhythmic precision."*
- **C4 — The question.** *"Is the scrolling cursor with proportional space/time enough and intuitive?"* — enough for the RHYTHM of a
  fast phrase, at the scroll speed, read by eye.
- **C5 — The two ways of reading time (the poles).** *"There is a tension between reacting and relying on the animated tools"*:
  - **the PILOT CONSOLE** — *"dials that give you state so you can adjust your behavior"*; the player REACTS to the page; the time is
    in the tools (the cursor, the pie, the tube, the curve)
  - **COUNTING** — *"you count the time 1st, this is in the foreground and you understand what to do on which count"*; the time is
    in the player (an internal map)
- **C6 — Analog vs digital.** *"The phrase flows over time"* — analog. *"The count is a fudge/digital, you work out 'sampling frames'
  and understand what state you are in at that count."* Temporal notation shows the FLOW (*"as in my cres curves"*) but is *"hard to
  describe fine details that change quickly"*. The precise instructions (Hübler / Ferneyhough) *"eventually work but are still
  counting"* — *"a very sophisticated internal time map with high resolution"*; Hübler adds *"multi tasking … several states at any
  moment"*.
- **C7 — The multitasking case in THIS piece.** *"The vibes morph where 1 vibraphone has to manage different dynamic levels and swells
  in each bow, non-synchronized"* — less weight for the EH, *"worth thinking thru"*.
- **C8 — The problem stated.** *"How to create a phrase in temporal notation with detailed changes, moment by moment changes in
  dynamic, articulations accents, timbre etc; what are the models? what is the best one here?"*
- **C9 — Two models of how a phrase is LEARNED and then PLAYED:**
  - **(F) Ferneyhough** — *"worked out in rehearsal and maybe pieced together layer by layer over counting, then internalized and
    eventually performed as a stream/phrase"* — the count is the scaffold, removed in performance
  - **(L) Lutosławski** — *"describe a phrase in notation that is either clear in the notation or can also be internalized but then
    played at different tempos, or in the absence of counting"* — the rhythm is exact INSIDE the phrase, the phrase's place in time
    is set by something else

*The AI's reading, marked (2026-09-29):* in both (F) and (L) the phrase is performed WITHOUT counting in the end; they differ in how
the rhythm reaches the player — (F) through the count, (L) through the shape. On a scrolling page the cursor already gives the
phrase's place in time (the pilot console's job), so the open question is only the rhythm INSIDE the phrase: whether distance alone
carries it at the scroll's resolution, or whether the phrase is written with values and beams (the ratios as a shape, (L)'s cell)
inside the temporal frame. A hypothesis for the talk, not a decision.

### §6a The frame — what a phrase notation carries, and how to compare two notations

**Four things every phrase notation has to carry, each of which can live in a different place:**

| | What | Kind of time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PLACEMENT** — when the phrase starts | absolute |
| 2 | **SPAN** — how long it lasts, when it ends | absolute (or free) |
| 3 | **SHAPE** — the rhythm inside: grouping, ratios, acceleration, the accent pattern | relative |
| 4 | **FLOW + MOMENTS** — the continuous parameters over the phrase (dynamic, timbre, pressure) and the discrete marks at a note | continuous · per note |

**Two axes to compare them on:**

- **Where the clock lives:** in the PAGE (a cursor, a seconds scale) · in the PLAYER (a count, an internal map) · in a CONDUCTOR
  (a cue) · in the ENSEMBLE (the ear). A notation can put the placement in one and the shape in another.
- **The resolution of the prescription** — Karkoschka's four kinds (1966): PRECISE · FRAME (*Rahmennotation*: the frame exact and the
  content approximate, or the reverse) · INDICATIVE · GRAPHIC; and Seeger's PRESCRIPTIVE vs DESCRIPTIVE writing (1958); and, for the
  detail, RESULT notation (the sound) vs ACTION notation (what the hand does — Lachenmann · Hübler).

### §6c The conceptual model PROPOSED — the phrase in layers (the AI's, marked; not decided)

**Principle 0 — the cursor's grain is the PHRASE, not the note.** Absolute time binds the phrase's anchor (and, if drawn, its span).
Everything inside the phrase is relative to the phrase.

| Layer | What | Time | Carrier | Read how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 THE ANCHOR** | where the phrase starts (its end if it matters) | absolute | the GC · the first head on its time · a span line | the pilot console: the cursor arrives |
| **2 THE SHAPE** | the rhythm inside — grouping, ratios, pace changes, bursts | relative | TN's shape devices: values · beams · tuplet brackets · feathered beams · grace groups | LEARNED as a gesture (Lutosławski · M2's principle 2); the count a practice-room scaffold only (Ferneyhough); never counted in performance |
| **3 THE FLOW** | the parameters that move continuously across the phrase — dynamic; position (sul pont …), pressure, embouchure timbre | continuous | LINES between states — the level curve as built (PLAN 2l), the same protocol for the others (Lachenmann) | the console: the tube shows the motion; the line shows the intention |
| **4 THE MOMENTS** | a discrete change AT a note — an accent, an articulation, a state reached | per note | a mark attached to a note of layer 2 — never to the clock | rides on the shape |

- **His tension resolved by the layers:** "describe the flow" is the DEFAULT — layers 1 … 3; "describe each moment" is layer 4, available
  per note, used sparingly. A parameter with a rhythm of its OWN (Hübler) gets its own line only where it really is independent — the
  vibraphone's two bows, not the EH's phrase.
- **What Q1 becomes:** the cursor is enough for layer 1 and layer 3, and NOT for layer 2 at speed — Berio is the precedent. Layer 2 needs
  the shape written. (A hypothesis; the measurement of Q1 still owed.)
- **The reading protocol, written for the performance notes:** learn the shape (count if you must); in performance, play it as a gesture
  from the anchor, while the console gives the flow. Counting is never in the foreground.
- **THE FIRST DESIGN CHOICE — named, not made:** inside a phrase, BOTH carriers (every head spatially true AND the shape written — M2's
  way; the cost: the fit and the dissonance rule) or THE ANCHOR ALONE spatially true, the shape carrying the interior (Lutosławski's way;
  the heads inside the phrase placed by the engraving, not the clock). It decides what the extractor does with a fast EH figure.
